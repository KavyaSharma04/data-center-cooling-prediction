Data Center Cooling Power Prediction

Project Motto

"From Guessing to Understanding: Smarter Cooling Predictions for Data Centers"

Cooling uses 30-40% of the electricity in a data center. This project predicts how much power the cooling system will need, hour by hour. It combines basic physics (how heat and humidity in the air behave) with deep learning models (ResNet and LSTM) that learn from past patterns. We tested it on 9.2 years of real data from a working data center, and it predicted cooling power with an average error of only about 2.5 kW.

Core Architectural Innovations

1. Deep Learning Tabular ResNet & LSTM Sequence Models

Overcomes tree-based boundary saturation with multi-layer skip connections, Batch Normalization, and GELU activations. The LSTM captures continuous thermal inertia and 24-hour diurnal thermal mass trajectories.

2. Domain Psychrometrics & Scale Invariance

Formulates Stull wet-bulb temperature, Magnus dew point, moist air enthalpy, and free-cooling economizer potential ($T_{wb} < 55^\circ ext{F}$). Solves the severe out-of-distribution IT workload shift by predicting scale-invariant cooling intensity.

3. Dual-Mode Deployment Architecture

Provides both an Exogenous Digital Twin Mode (pure physical simulation for capacity additions) and a Dynamic Telemetry-Aware Mode ($R^2 > 0.91$ for real-time BMS dispatch and peak shaving).

Discovery #1: 89.26% Out-of-Distribution Shift

Critical Barrier

In decision trees, splits are orthogonal ($x \le heta$). When the facility upgraded servers in 2023, IT load doubled. Nearly 90% of all test hours exceeded the maximum training value, forcing tree models to predict flat constant values.

Solution: Formulated the Physics-Guided Cooling Ratio ($ ext{cooling\_kw} / P_{ ext{IT}}$) and Deep Neural Networks with continuous linear activations to enable seamless out-of-bounds extrapolation.

Discovery #2: Temperature Correlation Inversion

Regime Shift

In 2016–2022, temperature correlation was negative ($pprox -0.30$). After the 2023 HVAC retrofit, mechanical chiller dependency inverted this relationship to strongly positive ($+0.60$ to $+0.75$).

Solution: Integrated Stull Wet-Bulb thermodynamics, free-cooling economizer proxies ($T_{wb} < 55^\circ ext{F}$), and thermal inertia lag windows to model physical cooling capacity directly.

Dynamic Dispatch vs Exogenous Digital Twin

Ablating target cooling lags causes the single most critical structural transformation in model behavior: R² drops from 0.9270 to 0.3644. This mathematically delineates two distinct operational regimes: (1) Exogenous Digital Twin (pure physical simulation for capacity planning without observing chiller meters) achieving R² ≈ 0.36, and (2) Closed-Loop Dispatch (real-time BMS feedforward control with prior-hour feedback) achieving R² ≈ 0.927.

Facility Thermal Capacitance & Memory

Ablating thermal inertia (lags, rolling averages, and rates-of-change) causes the catastrophic collapse to R² = 0.2212 (+269.8% MAE error increase). Data centers contain massive thermal storage in raised floor plenums, concrete slabs, and chilled water loops. Heat injected by servers at hour t requires 1 to 6 hours to be fully absorbed and rejected by chillers. Static snapshot models cannot capture this physical delay.

Deep Learning Requires Feature Engineering

When Tabular ResNet is trained on raw unengineered telemetry (the exact 9 features used by legacy models), its test R² is only 0.0363. Deep neural networks alone cannot resolve massive out-of-distribution workload shifts (+89.3% in test period) without domain physics and temporal memory. SOTA performance is driven by the symbiosis of deep residual representations and physical inductive biases.

Resilience Against Telemetry Sensor Outages

The ablation study demonstrates exceptional system resilience: If weather station telemetry completely disconnects (No Weather), the Hybrid Ensemble maintains R² = 0.9156 (+12.3% MAE change). If IT workload telemetry fails, the closed-loop model maintains R² = 0.9342. The multi-modal architecture gracefully degrades rather than catastrophically failing.
